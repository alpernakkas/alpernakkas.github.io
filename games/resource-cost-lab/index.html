Teaching guide & calculation notes

A 20-minute classroom exercise

  1. Inspect the dependency-only schedule. Is the opening date credible?
  2. Level resources. Explain why the finish and total cost change.
  3. Attempt the two-week challenge. Compare overtime and contractors.
  4. Introduce a disruption. Defend your revised decision to the sponsor.

What students should question

Can more people always shorten an activity? Is overtime sustainable? Are contractor productivity and handoffs realistic? What costs are missing?

The baseline uses the original case with resource leveling. Edits affect the scenario and alternatives, while the original baseline stays fixed.

How the model works

CPM uses forward and backward passes. Leveling schedules eligible activities by longest remaining dependency path, reserving contiguous resource slots. It is a feasible heuristic, not proof of an optimal schedule.

Labor is based on the original effort, with overtime and contractor premiums; overhead follows elapsed workdays. External approval cannot be accelerated. All figures are illustrative.

The comparison guide uses calculated schedules and costs. All calculations run in your browser; this version does not use a live AI service.
